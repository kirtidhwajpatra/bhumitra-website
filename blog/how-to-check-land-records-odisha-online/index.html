How to check land records in Odisha online (Bhulekh RoR guide)

A step-by-step guide to finding the Record of Rights for any plot in Odisha: by khata number, plot number or owner name on Bhulekh, or straight from the map when you don't know the numbers.

By the Bhumitra team · 29 October 2026 · 3 min read

Every piece of land in Odisha has an official record called the Record of Rights (RoR), also known as the khatiyan. It names the owners, gives the khata and plot numbers, the area and the type of land. The state publishes these records online through the Bhulekh portal, so you can check a plot from your phone without visiting an office.

This guide covers the three ways to find a record and how to choose between them.

What you need before you start

Records are filed by location. Whichever method you use, you will be asked for:

  • District, for example Khordha or Keonjhar.
  • Tahasil, the revenue sub-division that keeps the record.
  • Village (mouza), the revenue village. It is not always the same as the name people use locally.

After that you need one of: the khata number, the plot number, or the owner's name as it appears in the record.

Method 1: search Bhulekh by khata, plot or owner

  1. Open the Bhulekh portal at bhulekh.ori.nic.in.
  2. Choose the RoR view, then select district, tahasil and village.
  3. Pick how you want to search: by khatiyan (khata), by plot, or by tenant (owner) name.
  4. Select the number or name from the list and open the record.

This works well when you have a document in hand, such as an old sale deed or a tax receipt, that already shows the khata or plot number.

Method 2: when you only know where the land is

Most people know their land by sight: "the plot beside the canal, behind the school". Government portals start from numbers, which is where many searches get stuck. Owner-name search helps, but names are recorded in Odia and spellings vary, so it often takes several tries.

A map is easier. In Bhumitra you choose a village, or tap locate to use your GPS, and the official plot boundaries appear over satellite imagery. You tap the plot you're looking at and its Record of Rights opens. The plot number and khata come with it, so you can note them down for any later paperwork.

Method 3: manual search inside the app

If you do have a number, Bhumitra also has a manual search. Pick district, tahasil and village, then search by plot number, khata number or plot unique ID. The app checks the result against Bhulekh and marks it as found, unverified or not found, so you can tell a confirmed record from a partial one.

How to read what you find

An RoR packs a lot into a small table. The fields that matter most are the owners and their shares, the khata number, each plot's area and its kisam (land type), and the remarks column, which can note a mortgage, a court case or a pending change. Our guide to reading an Odisha RoR explains each field.

Online copy or certified copy?

An online record is the quickest way to check facts, but it is for information only. Banks, registration offices and courts usually ask for a certified copy from the Tahasil office. Use the online record to confirm you have the right plot and owner, then get the certified copy when you need to act on it.

Common problems

  • The village isn't listed. Revenue village names can differ from local names. Try nearby spellings, or find the village on the map instead.
  • The owner's name doesn't match. The record may still be in a parent's or grandparent's name if the land was inherited and never mutated.
  • The portal is slow or down. Bhulekh is busiest during office hours. Try again later; Bhumitra shows a clear message when the source is unavailable instead of showing old or made-up data.

Frequently asked questions

Is it free to check land records in Odisha?

Yes. The Bhulekh portal run by the Government of Odisha lets anyone view the Record of Rights online without charge. A certified copy for legal use is issued by the Tahasil office.

Can I check land records without the khata number?

Yes. On Bhulekh you can also search by plot number or by the owner's name inside a village. In Bhumitra you can open the plot map for a village and tap the plot itself, so you don't need any number.

Is an online RoR printout legally valid?

An online copy is good for checking information, but it is not a certified copy. For a sale, loan or court matter, get a certified RoR from the Tahasil office.
