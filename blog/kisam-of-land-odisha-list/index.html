Kisam of land in Odisha: list of land types and what they mean

A plain-English list of the kisam (land types) you'll see in an Odisha RoR: gharabari, sarad, bahal, berna, atta, bagayat, patita, gochar, anabadi and more, and why kisam matters.

Every plot in an Odisha Record of Rights has a kisam: the classification of the land. It's one short word in the RoR, but it decides what you can do with the land, what it's worth on paper, and whether you can build on it.

Why kisam matters

  • Use. Building a house normally needs homestead (gharabari) kisam. Agricultural land needs conversion first.
  • Value. The government benchmark value is set per kisam, so it drives stamp duty.
  • Red flags. Some kisams mark government or common land that a private person usually can't own or sell.

Common kisams in Odisha

Names are written in Odia in the RoR. These are the classifications you'll come across most often, grouped by type. Spellings in English vary.

Homestead and garden

KisamMeaning
GharabariHomestead: land under a house and its yard
BagayatGarden or orchard land

Agricultural land

KisamMeaning
Sarad (I, II, III)Paddy land, graded by quality, with grade I the best
BahalLow-lying paddy land that holds water
BernaMedium land, between low and upland
AttaUpland, higher and drier, often for non-paddy crops
Jalasechita / AnajalasechitaIrrigated / unirrigated land, often combined with a crop class
DofasaliLand that grows two crops a year

Fallow and waste land

KisamMeaning
PatitaFallow land, not under cultivation
Abada jogya anabadiGovernment land that could be cultivated but is unoccupied
Abada ajogya anabadiGovernment land not fit for cultivation

Common and public land

KisamMeaning
GocharVillage grazing land
RastaRoad or path
NalaDrain or stream
PokhariPond or tank
SmasanCremation or burial ground
JungleForest or scrub land

Kisam names and grades vary between districts and settlement years. Treat this list as a guide, and confirm the meaning for your plot with the Tahasil or a local advocate.

Kisam and the owner together

Read the kisam alongside the owner. A plot of gochar or rasta recorded in the government's name is public land. A private seller offering it is a serious warning sign. See how to tell government land from private land.

How to check a plot's kisam

  1. Open the plot's RoR on Bhulekh, or tap the plot on the map in Bhumitra.
  2. Find the plot in the list. Each plot row shows its own kisam and area.
  3. If a khata has several plots, check the kisam of the exact plot you care about. They can differ.

Bhumitra shows the kisam for each plot with its area, and uses it to pick the right government benchmark rate.

Changing a plot's kisam

A private owner changes agricultural kisam to a non-agricultural one through conversion under Section 8-A of the OLR Act. Read our land conversion guide for the process.

Frequently asked questions

What does kisam mean in Odisha land records?

Kisam is the classification of a plot in the Record of Rights. It records what kind of land it is, such as homestead, paddy land of a given grade, orchard, fallow, or government land like roads and grazing land.

What is patita land in Odisha?

Patita means fallow land, land that is not being cultivated. When the owner is the government it is usually recorded as anabadi (unoccupied) land.

What is gharabari kisam?

Gharabari is homestead land, the classification for land used for a house and its surroundings. Agricultural land is usually converted to gharabari before building a house.

How do I change the kisam of my land in Odisha?

Apply for conversion under Section 8-A of the Odisha Land Reforms Act to the Tahasildar. Once the case is allowed and the premium paid, the kisam in the RoR is updated.
