Record of Rights (RoR) in Odisha: what every field means

Khata, plot number, kisam, area in acres and decimals, owner shares and remarks. A field-by-field explanation of the Odisha RoR so you can read your land record with confidence.

The Record of Rights, or RoR, is the revenue department's official statement of who holds a piece of land and on what terms. In Odisha it is also called the khatiyan. It looks dense at first, but it follows the same structure for every village. Once you know the fields, you can read any record in a couple of minutes.

Location: district, tahasil, village, thana

The header names the district, tahasil, revenue village (mouza) and often the police station (thana) and its number. Check these first. Two villages with similar names in different tahasils are a common source of mix-ups.

Khata number

The khata is a holding: the group of plots held by the same owner, or by the same set of co-owners. One khata can hold one plot or dozens. When people say "send me the khata", they usually mean this whole record.

Plot number

Each plot is one parcel on the village cadastral map, with its own number. The plot number is what ties the paper record to a shape on the ground. In Bhumitra, tapping a plot on the map gives you this number and opens the khata it belongs to, along with the other plots in the same khata.

Owners and shares

The owner (tenant) section lists each recorded holder, usually with a father's or husband's name and sometimes a residence. Where land is jointly held, each person's share is shown as a fraction, such as 1/2 or 1/4. The shares add up to the whole holding. A buyer should make sure every co-owner with a share is part of a sale.

Area

Odisha records area in acres and decimals, where 100 decimals make one acre and one decimal is 435.6 sq ft. You may see a figure like 0.250, which is a quarter of an acre or 25 decimals. Our land units guide converts this to sq ft, guntha, bigha and other local units.

Kisam (land type)

Kisam is the classification of the land. Typical entries describe homestead land (gharabari), agricultural land with or without irrigation, orchards (bagayat), fallow land and government categories such as roads or grazing land. Kisam matters for two reasons:

  • It limits use. Building a house on land recorded as agricultural normally needs a change of land use (conversion) first.
  • It affects the government benchmark value, and so the stamp duty you pay at registration.

Remarks

Read the remarks column every time. This is where the record notes things like a mortgage to a bank or cooperative, an ongoing case, a partition, or a mutation that is in progress. An empty remarks column is good news. Bhumitra shows "No encumbrance or dispute noted in register" only when the register itself says so.

Other details you may see

  • Ownership type or status, such as stitiban (with permanent rights) or other tenure categories.
  • Land revenue (land tax) payable on the holding.
  • Final publication date of the settlement, which tells you how old the base record is.

What the RoR does not tell you

The RoR shows the current recorded position. It is not a full history of sales, and it does not guarantee there are no unregistered agreements. Before buying, also get an Encumbrance Certificate from the registration office, which lists registered transactions on the property. See our land buying checklist for the full list.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between khata and plot number?

A plot number identifies one piece of land on the village map. A khata number identifies a holding: all the plots owned together by the same owner or group of owners. One khata can contain many plots.

What does kisam mean in an Odisha RoR?

Kisam is the land classification recorded by the revenue department, such as homestead, irrigated agricultural land or orchard. It affects what the land can be used for and its government benchmark value.

Why does my RoR still show my grandfather's name?

Ownership in the RoR changes only after mutation. If land was inherited or sold and nobody applied for mutation at the Tahasil, the old owner's name stays on the record.
