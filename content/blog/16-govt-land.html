One of the most expensive mistakes in land buying is paying for a plot that turns out to be government land: part of a road, a canal bank, a village pond or grazing land. Brokers sometimes sell such land as private. The Record of Rights usually tells you the truth in a few seconds, if you know where to look.

Three things to read in the RoR

1. The owner entry

Private land names one or more people with their father's or husband's names and shares. Government land is recorded in the name of the state government (in the Odia record, Odisha Sarakar or similar).

2. The khata

Unallotted government land is commonly kept in an anabadi (unoccupied) khata. If the khata for your plot is anabadi, the land is not privately owned.

3. The kisam

Some kisams are used almost only for public land:

KisamWhat it is
GocharGrazing land for the village
RastaRoad or path
NalaDrain or stream
PokhariPond or tank
SmasanCremation or burial ground
Abada jogya / ajogya anabadiUnoccupied government land

Warning signs before you buy

  • The seller can't show a khata in their own name, only "possession".
  • The plot number on the deed doesn't match the plot you're shown on the ground.
  • The plot sits between a road and a canal, or along a riverbank or pond edge.
  • The price is far below the benchmark value of neighbouring private plots.
  • You're told a "patta is coming" or a "lease will be converted". Ask to see the order.

Leases and pattas

Government land can be settled or leased to people under the state's own rules, for example for homestead to eligible families. Such land comes with an official order and is then recorded in the beneficiary's name, often with conditions on sale. If a seller's title comes from a government grant, have an advocate check whether it can be transferred.

Check it on the map, then in the record

Roads, canals, ponds and grazing land are separate numbered plots on the village map. In Bhumitra, the plot lines sit over a satellite image, so you can see whether the "plot" you're being sold is actually part of a road or pond. Tap it to open its Record of Rights and read the owner and kisam. Then tap the neighbouring plots too.
