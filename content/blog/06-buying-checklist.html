For most families, a plot of land is the biggest purchase they'll make. Most problems that come up later, such as a missing co-owner, a boundary that doesn't match, or a loan nobody mentioned, could have been found before any money changed hands. Work through this list before you pay an advance.

1. Read the current Record of Rights

Open the RoR for the exact plot. Confirm the plot number, khata, area and kisam. If you don't know the numbers, find the plot on the map in Bhumitra and tap it. Our RoR guide explains each field.

2. Match every owner and share

Every person in the RoR with a share must agree to the sale and sign, or the seller must show how they got those shares (for example a registered partition). Ask for ID for each signatory and check the names, including father's names, against the record.

3. Walk the boundary against the map

Look at the plot on the official cadastral map over satellite imagery, then walk it. Do the bunds, fences, roads and canals line up with the plot lines? Is there access to a road on the map, not just a path across someone else's field? Map boundaries are for reference; for a precise boundary, ask the Revenue Inspector or a licensed surveyor to measure it.

4. Check the kisam and what you can build

If you plan to build a house and the land is recorded as agricultural, it will normally need conversion first. Ask whether conversion has been done and see the order. Government categories such as roads, grazing land or water bodies are a red flag.

5. Read the remarks column

Notes about a mortgage, a case, a partition or a pending mutation appear here. Ask the seller to explain anything you see, and get it in writing.

6. Get an Encumbrance Certificate

The RoR shows the current position, not the history. An Encumbrance Certificate from the registration office lists registered transactions on the property over the years you ask for, such as earlier sales and mortgages. Ask for at least the last 13 to 30 years where possible.

7. See the chain of title deeds

Ask for the seller's deed and the earlier deeds or inheritance documents that show how the land came to them. Bhumitra's land report also shows registered sales for a plot where they are available online, which is a quick cross-check.

8. Compare the price with the benchmark value

Look up the government benchmark value for the plot and its kisam. A price far below it is worth questioning. The benchmark also sets the minimum value for stamp duty, so add the registration costs to your budget now.

9. Confirm the area in your unit

Convert the recorded area into the unit the seller is quoting before agreeing a price. See the Odisha land units table: 1 decimal is 435.6 sq ft.

10. Plan the mutation

Registration transfers the land to you, but the RoR changes only after mutation at the Tahasil. Apply soon after registration and keep checking the record until your name appears.

Get help where it matters

Tools like Bhumitra make the first checks fast. You can do most of points 1 to 5, 8 and 9 from your phone in a few minutes, save the plot and share it with family. For the deeds, the Encumbrance Certificate and the final paperwork, work with a local advocate who handles land matters.
