"Bhulekh Odisha plot details by name" is one of the most searched land questions in the state, and for good reason. Most people don't have a khata or plot number to hand, but they do know whose land it is. Bhulekh lets you search the Record of Rights (RoR) by the owner's name, which the portal calls the tenant.

This guide walks through the search, explains why it often comes up empty, and shows what to do instead.

What you need before searching by name

Bhulekh does not search the whole state at once. You must narrow it down to one revenue village:

If you don't know the tahasil, look at an old tax receipt, ask a neighbour, or find the village on a map first (see below).

Step-by-step: search by name on Bhulekh

  1. Open bhulekh.ori.nic.in and choose RoR View.
  2. Select the district, then the tahasil, then the village.
  3. Choose the search type Tenant (owner name) instead of Khatiyan or Plot.
  4. Pick the name from the list. The portal shows names in Odia, sorted alphabetically.
  5. Open the matching khata to see the full RoR: all owners with shares, every plot in the holding, area and kisam.

Bhulekh times out if you use the browser's Back or Refresh button, or click a button twice. If you see a "timed out" page, start again from the home page.

Why name searches fail, and how to fix them

ProblemWhat to try
Name not in the listNames are recorded in Odia. Look for the first letter in Odia and scan nearby entries; spellings like "Mohapatra" and "Mahapatra" sort differently.
Too many similar namesOpen each khata and check the father's or husband's name printed with the owner.
Land was inheritedSearch the parent's or grandparent's name. If mutation was never done, the record is still in their name.
Wrong villageThe revenue village can differ from the local name or postal address. Check neighbouring villages in the same tahasil.
Recent saleIf the land was sold recently, the record may still show the seller until mutation is complete.

Faster: find the plot on the map instead

A name search works only if you know the exact revenue village and the spelling in the record. If you know where the land is, a map is quicker. In Bhumitra, tap locate or search the village, and the official plot boundaries appear over satellite imagery. Tap the plot and its RoR opens, with the owners, khata and plot number. You can then confirm the owner's name by reading the record, instead of guessing how it's spelled.

Bhumitra also has a manual search by plot number, khata number or plot unique ID once you have any of those.

What to check once you find the record

  • All co-owners and shares. A khata often has several owners. Every one with a share matters if land is being sold.
  • Plot list. One khata may hold many plots. Make sure the plot you care about is in it.
  • Kisam. The land type decides what the land can be used for. See our kisam list.
  • Remarks. Mortgages, cases and pending mutations are noted here.

About "PDF" downloads by name

Many people search for "land record by name Odisha PDF". Bhulekh shows the RoR as a page you can print or save as PDF from your browser. That copy is for reference only. For a bank, court or registration office, you need a certified copy from the Tahasil.
