Khajana is the land revenue an owner pays to the state on a holding. It's a small amount for most families, but the receipt matters more than the money. Tahasils and banks routinely ask for it, and an unpaid balance can hold up a mutation or conversion case.

Odisha lets you pay it online through e-Pauti (ଇ-ପାଉତି), run by the Revenue and Disaster Management Department.

What you need

  • District, tahasil and village of the land.
  • The khata number. If you don't have it, find it from the RoR first (see how to find your khata number).
  • A UPI app, debit card or net banking.

Pay khajana online, step by step

  1. Open odishalandrevenue.nic.in.
  2. Choose Pay Land Revenue.
  3. Select the district, tahasil and village, then enter or pick the khata.
  4. Check that the owner and khata match your RoR before paying.
  5. Pay online and download the receipt. Save it somewhere safe; you'll be asked for it again.

Check payment status or download an old receipt

On e-Pauti, choose Know Payment Status & Download Receipt. This is useful if the payment went through but the receipt didn't download, or if you need a copy for an application.

e-Pauti posts notices about scheduled maintenance on its home page. If the service is down, try again after the stated time.

Common problems

  • Khata not found. Check the village. Revenue village names can differ from local names.
  • Old owner's name shows. The RoR hasn't been mutated yet. You can usually still pay, but start the mutation so the record is in your name.
  • Payment debited, no receipt. Wait a while, then use Know Payment Status. Keep your bank reference number.

Keep your land records together

Save the khata and plot numbers from your receipt in Bhumitra. Once the plot is saved, it opens again with one tap, along with its RoR, government value and registration cost estimate.
