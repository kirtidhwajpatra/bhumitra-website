Every plot in an Odisha Record of Rights has a kisam: the classification of the land. It's one short word in the RoR, but it decides what you can do with the land, what it's worth on paper, and whether you can build on it.

Why kisam matters

  • Use. Building a house normally needs homestead (gharabari) kisam. Agricultural land needs conversion first.
  • Value. The government benchmark value is set per kisam, so it drives stamp duty.
  • Red flags. Some kisams mark government or common land that a private person usually can't own or sell.

Common kisams in Odisha

Names are written in Odia in the RoR. These are the classifications you'll come across most often, grouped by type. Spellings in English vary.

Homestead and garden

KisamMeaning
GharabariHomestead: land under a house and its yard
BagayatGarden or orchard land

Agricultural land

Kisam names and grades vary between districts and settlement years. Treat this list as a guide, and confirm the meaning for your plot with the Tahasil or a local advocate.

Kisam and the owner together

Read the kisam alongside the owner. A plot of gochar or rasta recorded in the government's name is public land. A private seller offering it is a serious warning sign. See how to tell government land from private land.

How to check a plot's kisam

  1. Open the plot's RoR on Bhulekh, or tap the plot on the map in Bhumitra.
  2. Find the plot in the list. Each plot row shows its own kisam and area.
  3. If a khata has several plots, check the kisam of the exact plot you care about. They can differ.

Bhumitra shows the kisam for each plot with its area, and uses it to pick the right government benchmark rate.

Changing a plot's kisam

A private owner changes agricultural kisam to a non-agricultural one through conversion under Section 8-A of the OLR Act. Read our land conversion guide for the process.
