A Record of Rights tells you who owns a plot. The village map tells you where that plot is and what shape it has. In Odisha, the cadastral (revenue) maps for most villages are online, and you can look up a plot's map from your phone.

Bhulekh Map View, step by step

  1. Go to bhulekh.ori.nic.in and choose Map View.
  2. Select district, tahasil and village.
  3. Enter or pick the plot number. The plot is highlighted on the village map.
  4. Zoom in to see neighbouring plot numbers. These help you confirm you're looking at the right parcel.

BhuNaksha Odisha, step by step

  1. Open bhunakshaodisha.nic.in.
  2. Select the district, then work down through tahasil, RI circle and village.
  3. Click a plot on the map, or search its number, to see the plot details and a sketch.

BhuNaksha states that its maps are "for the purpose of viewing only" and not for legal use. The same applies to every online map, including Bhumitra's.

How Odisha's land map is organised

Because plot numbers restart in every village, "plot 450" means nothing on its own. Always note the village and tahasil with it.

The hard part: matching map lines to the ground

A village map is a line drawing. In the field you see bunds, trees, a canal and a road, not plot numbers. That's why many people can open the map but still can't say which plot they're standing on.

Bhumitra lays the official plot lines over a satellite image. You can see the plot boundaries over real fields, roads and houses, and the app can centre the map on your GPS position. Tap a plot to open its Record of Rights with the owners, khata, area and kisam.

Phone GPS is usually accurate to within a few metres outdoors. That's enough to pick the right plot in most fields, but not enough to settle a boundary to the foot.

Tips for reading a plot map

  • Check neighbours. Note the plot numbers on each side. They are often listed in sale deeds as the boundary description.
  • Look for road access. Make sure the plot touches a road or path on the map, not just in practice.
  • Watch for splits. After a partition or sale, a plot may be divided into sub-plots with numbers like 450/1234. Older maps may not show the split yet.
  • Government plots nearby. Roads, canals and grazing land are separate government plots. Building over them causes problems later. See how to tell government land from private land.
