Government land portals almost always ask for a khata or plot number first. That's a problem when the documents are with a relative, lost in a move, or were never handed over after an inheritance. Here are the ways to recover the numbers, starting with the quickest.

1. Check the papers you may already have

These documents usually carry the numbers:

  • Land revenue (khajana) receipt. Shows the khata number and often the plot numbers.
  • Sale deed. The property schedule near the end lists village, khata and plot.
  • Old RoR copy or mutation order. Both name the khata directly.
  • Bank loan papers where the land was used as security.

2. Find the plot on the map

If you can stand on the land, or point to it on a satellite view, you don't need any paper. In Bhumitra:

  1. Tap locate. The app works out your district, tahasil and village from GPS. You can also search the village by name.
  2. The village's official plot lines appear over the satellite image.
  3. Tap the plot. The plot number, khata number, area and owners open from the Record of Rights.

The boundaries come from the state's cadastral maps, so match them against what you see on the ground, such as bunds, roads and canals. GPS on a phone is usually accurate to a few metres, which is enough to pick the right plot in most fields but not to settle a boundary dispute.

3. Search by owner name

On Bhulekh you can search a village's record by tenant (owner) name. Records are written in Odia, so try the name as it would appear in the record, including the father's name if the list is long. If the land was inherited, search for the earlier owner too.

4. Ask a neighbour for their plot number

Plot numbers on a village map run in sequence across neighbouring parcels. If the next-door owner knows their number, open their plot and look at the adjacent plots on the map. This often gets you to the right parcel in a minute or two.

5. Visit the Tahasil or RI office

The Revenue Inspector (RI) and the Tahasil office hold the village maps and registers. Staff can find a plot from a description and nearby landmarks. It takes longer, but it's the authoritative route and you can get a certified copy at the same time.

Once you have the numbers

Save them. In Bhumitra you can save a plot to your list so it opens again with one tap, and share the record with family or a lawyer. Then read the record carefully using our RoR field guide.
